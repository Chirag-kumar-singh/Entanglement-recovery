# D5.21 — Cross-Seed Validation of Recovery-Exit Tracking Error

## Objective

Test whether the cap-44-vs-cap-48 tracking-error/drop relationship
found for seed 0 in D5.20 generalizes across the existing 10-seed
cohort (D5.19/D5.5). Diagnostic only — not a search for a new
production cap.

## 1. Inspection before running

Reviewed D5.19 and D5.20 reports and source (`_update_fsm`,
`_apply_hybrid_actuators`, `ResidualRecoveryActionCfg`) again before
designing this run. Confirmed (as in D5.20) that `max_recovery_steps`
only changes *when* `exiting_recovery` fires
(`genuine_recovery_complete | cap_reached`), never what the actuator
restoration or crossing-detection logic does, and that the trigger
step and stage-1/2 crossing latch steps are purely sensor/geometry
driven and therefore identical between the cap-44 and cap-48
conditions for a given seed (confirmed empirically below — stage1/
stage2 latch steps match exactly between conditions in every seed
where cap 44 is reached after the latch).

## 2. Reuse of existing D5.19 logs as the cap-48 baseline

D5.19's `logs/d5_19_handoff_error_analysis/seed{0..9}/trajectory.csv`
were generated by the same unmodified
`scripts/d5_18_post_recovery_diagnostic.py`, same seed values, same
default `max_recovery_steps_override=48`, same frozen checkpoint, wire
geometry, and FL-leg configuration used throughout D5.16-D5.20 — i.e.
identical configuration to D5.20's `cap48` control. These were reused
**unmodified, without rerunning**, as the cap-48 side of every pair.

## 3. New runs: cap 44, seeds 0-9

Ran only the cap-44 condition (per instructions, not 40/56/64 — D5.20
already established 56/64 are no-ops and 40 is confounded/too severe
to be a clean pairwise comparison at the single seed tested) for all
10 seeds, using the exact same unmodified script and otherwise
identical configuration (seed, checkpoint, wire geometry, hip-wire
setting, sim config, actuator commands):

```
uv run python scripts/d5_18_post_recovery_diagnostic.py --seed <N> --max-steps 200 \
  --max-recovery-steps-override 44 --out-dir logs/d5_21_cross_seed_handoff_validation/seed<N>_cap44
```

for N in 0..9. All 10 outputs are new directories under
`logs/d5_21_cross_seed_handoff_validation/`; no existing log was
overwritten.

**Tracking-error unit verification:** as in D5.19/D5.20, `pos_thigh_
ctrl_target`/`pos_calf_ctrl_target` are read from `env.sim.data.ctrl`
*after* the existing `JointPositionAction` scale/offset pipeline has
executed, so they are already in the same physical joint-radian units
as `thigh_pos`/`calf_pos` (`qpos`) — confirmed from source, not
assumed; `target − actual` requires no further conversion.

### Separating cap-48-success seeds from cap-48-already-failing seeds

6/10 seeds (0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 8) achieve `genuine_recovery_complete=True`
at cap 48. 4/10 (5, 6, 7, 9) are **already** `cap_timeout` failures at
cap 48 (confirmed, from D5.19), so for these four the "cap48 vs cap44"
comparison is a failure-vs-failure comparison, not a
success-vs-failure comparison — both conditions fail to cross for
these seeds, and the shrinking of their already-failed-run's measured
error/drop at the even-shorter cap must not be read as evidence that
a shorter cap "helps" (both outcomes are failures; the task explicitly
warns against this interpretation).

## 5. Cross-seed test of the tracking-error / dip relationship

Pearson r(Δ|thigh_err|, Δdrop) across all 10 seeds = **0.912**;
restricted to the 6 cap-48-genuine seeds only = **0.949**. Both are
strong positive correlations, consistent with D5.20's single-seed
finding generalizing in direction across the cohort: seeds with a
larger error reduction at cap 44 also show a larger (or equal, at the
floor) drop reduction.

**However, with n=10 (and n=6 in the success-only subset), per
instructions, this correlation coefficient is not treated as
confirmatory on its own.** Several qualitative patterns are more
informative than the single r value:

- Three seeds (3, 4, 7) show **zero** drop at *both* caps — a floor
  effect that mechanically inflates agreement (zero vs. zero is a
  "match" regardless of whether the underlying mechanism is tracking
  error).
- The two seeds with the mildest error reduction (0: Δ=0.244, 8:
  Δ=0.204) also show the smallest (nonzero) drop reduction (0.0086,
  0.0103) — a genuine graded match in a regime where neither condition
  hits the floor. This pair (0, 8) is the cohort's cleanest evidence
  for a graded (not just floor-driven) relationship.
- The four seeds with the largest error reduction (1, 2, 6, 9; Δ ≈
  0.97-1.18) all drop to **zero** measured dip at cap 44 — but see
  point 6 below: these are also the seeds where cap 44 coincides with
  or falls very close to the stage-1/2 crossing-latch step itself
  (severe relative truncation), not merely "slightly before" the
  natural exit as in seed 0's D5.20 case.

## 6. Alternative explanations: foot velocity, touchdown timing, whole-body state

Checking `foot_vx_base` (FL foot forward velocity, base-relative) at
the exit step reveals that **cap 44 does not represent a uniformly
"mildly earlier" version of the same handoff state across seeds** —
the absolute cap value of 44 lands at very different *relative*
positions within each seed's own RECOVERY timeline, because trigger
timing and stage-1/2 latch timing vary by seed (confirmed from the
per-seed `stage1_first_latch_step`/`stage2_first_latch_step` values,
which are themselves cap-independent, sensor-driven quantities, and
differ across seeds from step 82 to step 91):

| seed | cap48 foot_vx_base | cap44 foot_vx_base | cap44 lands at/near latch step? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | −2.227 | −2.166 | no (latch@84, exit@87, 3-step gap) |
| 8 | −2.287 | −2.183 | no (latch@83, exit@86, 3-step gap) |
| 1 | −2.084 | **+0.457** (sign flip) | **yes** (latch@86 = exit@86, 0-step gap) |
| 2 | −2.307 | −0.247 (near zero) | **yes** (latch@86 = exit@86, 0-step gap) |
| 6 | −2.412 | −0.500 | latch never achieved at cap48; cap44 exits before any latch |
| 9 | −2.204 | −0.402 | latch@87 = exit@87, 0-step gap |

This is the same confound D5.20 identified for its cap=40 seed-0
condition: when the cap forces an exit at or before the crossing
latch, the FL foot is caught in a qualitatively different (earlier,
still-forward-swinging) part of its trajectory, not simply "the same
trajectory, retracted slightly less." Seeds 1, 2, 6, 9 — exactly the
four seeds showing the largest error reduction and a drop-to-zero —
fall into this confounded regime. Seeds 0 and 8 — the cleanest,
smallest-Δ pair — do **not** (cap 44 still lands several steps after
the latch, in the same qualitative late-recovery regime as cap 48).

**This means the strong overall correlation is driven substantially by
a touchdown-timing/whole-body-state confound, not purely by a smooth
function of tracking-error magnitude.** The two seeds free of this
confound (0, 8) still show the predicted direction, but with
proportionally smaller, graded effects — this is the more trustworthy
subset for the tracking-error hypothesis specifically.

Ground-contact loss across all four feet is, as in every prior D5.x
diagnostic in this series, **universal** in both conditions for all
10 seeds — it does not discriminate between large- and small-drop
pairs and offers no additional explanatory power here.

## 7. Evidence vs. proof of causation

This cross-seed replication strengthens the *evidence that handoff
state (error magnitude and/or foot-velocity/touchdown-phase state)
predicts* the post-exit dip — the direction of the effect replicates
across 10 seeds, with the two most confound-free seeds (0, 8) showing
smaller but still consistent graded changes. It does **not** prove
that tracking error *itself*, independent of the touchdown-timing
confound identified in Section 6, is the causal mechanism: for 4 of
the 10 seeds, the "error reduction" and "drop reduction" occur
together with a qualitative change in which part of the foot's swing
trajectory is caught at exit, which is at least as plausible a
candidate explanation for the reduced instability as the tracking
error magnitude per se. Separating these two explanations would
require an intervention that holds touchdown/swing-phase state fixed
while varying tracking-error magnitude (or vice versa), which this
design cannot do with only the single `max_recovery_steps` lever.

## Limitations

- n=10 seeds (6 success-at-cap48, 4 already-failing-at-cap48); no
  significance test is reported, consistent with instructions for a
  small cohort.
- 3/10 seeds exhibit a floor effect (zero drop at both caps),
  reducing the effective graded-comparison sample to 7 seeds, and
  further to 2 (seeds 0, 8) once the touchdown-timing confound
  (Section 6) is excluded.
- Only two cap values (44, 48) were compared, per instructions not to
  rerun 40/56/64; this cannot fully characterize the relationship's
  shape, only its direction at this one additional comparison point.
- The absolute cap value of 44 is not matched in *relative* severity
  across seeds (it lands anywhere from "well past the crossing latch"
  to "exactly at or before the latch," depending on each seed's own
  timing) — this is the central caveat of this experiment and likely
  explains most of the apparent strength of the raw correlation.
- No viewer/visual validation was performed (same justification as
  D5.20 — pure parameter/logging reuse of already-validated code).

## Recommended next experiment (exactly one)

Repeat this same cap-44-vs-cap-48 paired design, but using a
**per-seed relative cap** (e.g., each seed's own measured
stage-1/2 latch elapsed time + a small fixed margin, such as
latch_elapsed + 2) instead of one fixed absolute cap value across all
seeds, so that the relative severity of the induced truncation is
matched across seeds rather than confounded with each seed's own
timing. This would isolate the tracking-error-magnitude effect from
the touchdown-timing/whole-body-state confound identified in Section 6,
using only the existing `max_recovery_steps` override (computed
per-seed from already-logged, sensor-driven latch steps) and no new
controller mechanism. This is **not implemented** in this task, per
instructions to stop after the analysis.
