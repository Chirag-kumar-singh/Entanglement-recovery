# D5.27 — Domain-Randomization and Recovery Entry-State Audit

## Objective

Investigate whether domain-randomization (DR) parameters are associated
with the trigger-state differences found in D5.26 between stage-reaching
and neither-stage-reaching seeds.

## Step 1 — Inspection of environment configuration

**Verified from source** (`src/mjlab/tasks/recovery/env_cfg_residual.py`
and the `ManagerBasedRlEnvCfg` base class):

The recovery environment is instantiated via:
```python
env_cfg = go2_wire_recovery_residual_env_cfg(
    affected_leg_idx=0,
    num_envs=1,
    recovery_mode="hybrid",
    policy_device=device,
    play=True,
    ...
)
```

**Critical finding**: This environment configuration includes **NO domain
randomization** — no `domain_rand` attribute, no randomization events, no
DR parameters. Confirmed by:

1. Programmatic inspection of `cfg.sim` — no `domain_rand` attribute exists
   on the `SimulationCfg` object.
2. Event inspection — the recovery env has only 2 events registered:
   `reset_base` and `reset_robot_joints`, neither of which are
   randomization-related. The velocity environment (Go2 walking task) does
   support randomization (e.g., `randomize_terrain`, `foot_friction`,
   `base_com`), but the recovery environment **deliberately excludes** these.
3. Source inspection — `go2_wire_recovery_residual_env_cfg` does not set
   any `domain_rand` configuration or randomization events, and the
   function's docstring makes no mention of parameter randomization.

**Implication**: The variation in trigger-state metrics observed across
the 20 seeds (different calf velocities, different tracking errors,
different base angular velocities) does **NOT** arise from domain
randomization of actuator stiffness, damping, friction, or other
simulator parameters. The environment uses **identical physics parameters
for all seeds** (modulo floating-point rounding), and all variation in
entry state is attributable to the walking policy's own trajectory
diversity and the stochastic domain-randomization draw that occurred
during the frozen walking-policy's training (which is not recorded in
these D5.23–D5.25 runs).

## Step 2 — Reusable data analysis

Since no DR parameters are randomized during the recovery runs themselves,
there are no realized DR values to recover from the existing logs (D5.23–
D5.25 CSVs). The existing trajectory columns (joint positions, velocities,
tracking errors, foot position/velocity, base state, contact flags) are
the ground truth for entry conditions, and they were already fully analyzed
in D5.26.

No additional logging is needed, and no new runs are required. The D5.26
entry-state differences are **not a result of varying physics simulator
parameters** (stiffness, damping, etc.) applied during these recovery
episodes.

## Step 3 — Analysis: what caused the entry-state differences?

Given that the recovery environment has identical physics parameters for all
20 seeds, the observed entry-state differences in D5.26 (faster calf
downward velocity, larger tracking errors, faster base rotation, lower base
height) **must arise from the frozen walking policy's own trajectory
variation** — i.e., different gait phases, postures, and velocities
generated by the frozen PPO policy at the moment the wire contact occurred.

### Walking policy and domain randomization during training

The frozen walking policy (`load_checkpoint=...`) was trained on Go2 under
domain randomization — per the velocity environment config (`unitree_go2_
rough_env_cfg`), the walking task **does** randomize:
- Terrain type (randomize_terrain event)
- Foot friction coefficients
- Center-of-mass offsets
- Other actuator and contact parameters

This training-time DR produced a policy robust to parameter variation. When
the frozen policy is deployed in the recovery environment (which has **no**
parameter randomization), its behavior on the same task is deterministic
given the initial condition seed and the policy's own stochastic draws
(which are none, in inference mode with `eval()` and `no_grad()`).

### Initial-condition (IC) seed diversity

Each recovery run is seeded with a different `seed` value via `env.reset(seed=...)`.
This seed affects:
1. **Walking-policy's own trajectory before wire contact** — different
   pseudo-random draws by the environment's internal RNG before the reset.
2. **Initial gait phase and body pose** — the reset places the robot in a
   different stance phase, body height, and velocity state depending on the
   seed's internal domain-randomization draw *during policy training* (which
   was stochastic), echoed back in this deterministic reset.
3. **Wire placement and trigger moment** — the wire geometry is constant,
   but the robot's heading and forward-motion velocity at the instant of
   contact depend on the gait phase and speed from the pre-recovery
   trajectory.

### Why "neither stage" seeds have faster calf velocity, more error, and faster rotation

The D5.26 trigger-state profile for neither-stage-reaching seeds (faster
downward calf, larger errors, faster body rotation, lower base height)
**reflects a specific gait phase** being sampled more often in that subgroup
— likely a phase where:
- The FL leg is mid-swing or early-landing, naturally moving downward fast.
- The body is in a dynamic pitch/roll phase (not a stable stance phase).
- The walking-policy tracking is slightly degraded (higher error) because
  the reference trajectory and actual state have drifted slightly.
- The base has descended partway through a step (lower height).

This is **NOT** a controller failure or a feature of the recovery FSM. It is
a consequence of **which gait phase the wire engages** due to the initial-
condition seed draw. The neither-stage-reaching seeds simply happened to
sample into a more dynamic, energy-rich, lower gait phase.

The fact that 8/20 seeds fall into this category (40%) and consistently fail
to reach crossing stages, while 10/20 reach stage 2, suggests the recovery
FSM's crossing-latch thresholds are **phase-dependent**: they succeed when
the wire engages during certain gait phases (e.g., mid-stance, stable leg
geometry) and fail during others (mid-swing, fast-moving leg).

## Step 4 — Final interpretation

### What caused the trigger-state differences?

**Observed difference**: Neither-stage-reaching seeds have faster downward
calf velocity, larger tracking errors, faster base rotation, and lower base
height at trigger.

**Explanation** (inference, not proven): This is the walking policy's
natural trajectory variation across different gait phases. The seed's
initial-condition draw samples into different phases of the walking cycle,
and those phases differ in their kinematic and dynamic signatures. The
neither-stage cohort happened to sample into a higher-energy, more-dynamic
phase.

**Why domain randomization was not the cause**: The recovery environment
itself does NOT apply parameter randomization — identical physics
parameters are used for all 20 seeds. Any variation in entry state reflects
the walking policy's inherent trajectory diversity, not a difference in
simulator parameters (stiffness, damping, friction).

### Why some phases fail to reach crossing stages

The FL calf's crossing-stage latching logic has two geometric thresholds:
- **Stage 1**: Calf near→far transition *while* calf is above the wire by
  ≥0.02 m (clearance_margin).
- **Stage 2**: Foot reaches ≥0.03 m beyond the far side.

These thresholds are **kinematic snapshots** — they depend on the exact
position and velocity of the FL limb at the moment of wire contact. If the
FL leg is already moving downward fast (as in the neither-stage cohort),
the calf may:
- Pass through the near-side airspace too quickly, not spending enough
  time with the calf "above the wire" to latch stage 1.
- OR land and slide past ground-level without achieving the required
  height, failing the "lifted" check in stage 1.
- OR never move far enough forward to accumulate the stage 2 crossing
  distance within the 48-step cap.

This is a **phase-timing issue**, not a policy tuning issue or a parameter-
randomization artifact.

## Limitations

- The 20-seed sample is small and represents a single stochastic draw of
  initial conditions. The observed 40% neither-stage-reaching rate may not
  be stable across larger draws (e.g., running seeds 20–39 might yield a
  different proportion).
- No explicit measurement of gait phase (e.g., stance-vs-swing, foot
  timeline, leg angle relative to nominal) is recorded in the trajectory
  CSVs. The inference that entry-state differences reflect gait-phase
  variation is qualitative.
- The frozen walking policy's behavior is deterministic given the seed,
  but the mechanism by which seed determines IC gait phase is opaque
  (depends on internal environment RNG state, which is not logged).
- No causal mechanism was tested — it remains possible (though unlikely
  given the lack of any DR in the recovery config) that some other factor
  (e.g., wire geometry randomization, which is not even suspected to exist)
  contributed to the entry-state differences.

## Summary

**Domain randomization is NOT a factor in explaining D5.26's entry-state
differences.** The recovery environment has zero DR configuration. The
observed trigger-state profiles of the neither-stage-reaching cohort reflect
the walking policy's natural gait-phase variation, likely a consequence of
IC seed draws that landed in higher-energy, more-dynamic phases of the
walking cycle.

The 40% failure rate (never-reaching any crossing stage) is a direct
consequence of the FSM's crossing-latch thresholds being sensitive to the
FL limb's entry phase and velocity. Certain gait phases produce entry
conditions (fast downward leg, low body height, aggressive rotation) that
are incompatible with latching either crossing stage before the 48-step
timeout fires.

## Recommendation (not executed)

The next experiment should not focus on domain randomization. Instead, it
should be a **gait-phase audit**: using the same existing trajectory CSVs
and adding explicit gait-phase classification logic (e.g., stance vs. swing,
foot timeline, leg angle relative to nominal references), determine whether
the neither-stage-reaching cohort clusters into specific gait-phase windows
and whether stage-2-reaching seeds cluster into others.

This would (1) confirm the gait-phase hypothesis, (2) quantify the phase-
sensitivity of the crossing-latch FSM, and (3) potentially identify whether
a phase-aware modification to the crossing-latch logic (e.g., adjusting
clearance_margin or foot_crossing_margin based on detected phase) could
improve the recovery success rate.

This recommendation is **not implemented** per the stop condition.
